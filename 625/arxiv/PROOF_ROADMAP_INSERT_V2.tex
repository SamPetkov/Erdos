\section{Proof architecture}\label{sec:proof-architecture}

The proof consists of three logically distinct stages: a comparison of two
first-moment locations, a normalized second-moment estimate for a signed
four-size profile, and a concentration argument that turns a
positive-probability witness into a high-probability cocoloring.  This section
records the dependency structure and the exact quantities that connect the
three stages.

\subsection{The two first-moment roots}

Let $r_+(n)$ be the continuous root of the ordinary profile first moment, and
let $r_4^{\mathrm{co}}(n)$ be the root of the signed objective restricted to
the four deficits $2,3,4,5$.  If $\delta_n$ denotes the phase parameter, put
\[
  A_4(\delta):=\log 2-D_4(\delta),
\]
where $D_4(\delta)$ is the entropy loss caused by restricting the deficit
support to these four values.  The first-moment calculation gives
\[
  r_+(n)-r_4^{\mathrm{co}}(n)
  =
  \left[
    \frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}A_4(\delta_n)+o(1)
  \right]
  \frac{n}{(\log n)^3}.
\]
We place the signed witness at the midpoint
\[
  k_{\mathrm{co}}
  :=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{r_4^{\mathrm{co}}(n)+r_+(n)}{2}
  \right\rceil.
\]
Therefore
\[
  r_+(n)-k_{\mathrm{co}}
  =
  \left[
    \frac{(\log 2)^2}{8}A_4(\delta_n)+o(1)
  \right]
  \frac{n}{(\log n)^3}.
\]
The entropy certificate in Section~5 yields the uniform bound
\[
  A_4(\delta)>
  \log\!\left(\frac{1000}{639}\right)
  \qquad (0\le \delta\le 1).
\]
Thus the first-moment calculation already supplies the constant in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-v2}.  The remaining task is to prove that the signed
profile is realized with sufficiently large probability.

\subsection{The exact signed-overlap identity}

Consider two ordered signed profile partitions, and let
$r=(r_{ab})$ be their overlap table.  Define $H_r$ to be the simple bipartite
graph whose edges are the cells with $r_{ab}\ge 2$.  Summing over the two sign
vectors gives the exact factorization
\[
  \operatorname{wt}_{\mathrm{sign}}(r)
  =
  \left(\prod_{a,b}g(r_{ab})\right)2^{\beta(H_r)},
\]
where $\beta(H_r)$ is the dimension of the binary cycle space.  The normalized
second moment is then decomposed into three parts: partial diagonals, the
canonical high-cell skeleton, and the residual attachment.

Section~7 controls the partial diagonals.  For the remaining overlap tables,
every cell above one half of the phase cap is unique in its row and column.
Consequently the positive high-cell support is a block-level matching.

\subsection{High cells, deficits, and physical fibers}

Fix a block matching $P$.  For $e\in P$, let $s_e$ and $t_e$ be the endpoint
block sizes, and define
\[
  m_e:=\min\{s_e,t_e\},
  \qquad
  j_e:=m_e-h_e.
\]
Here $j_e$ is the actual high multiplicity and $h_e$ is its deficit from full
containment.  Put
\[
  J:=\sum_{e\in P}j_e,
  \qquad
  H:=\sum_{e\in P}h_e.
\]
After summing the complete finite fiber of local partial stub matchings, the
aggregate bare weight is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-aggregate-high-weight}
  w(P,j)
  =
  \frac{
    \displaystyle\prod_{e\in P}(s_e)_{j_e}(t_e)_{j_e}
  }{
    \displaystyle (n)_J\prod_{e\in P}j_e!
  }
  \prod_{e\in P}g(j_e).
\end{equation}
No full completion is chosen for an individual partial matching.  Equation
\eqref{eq:v2-aggregate-high-weight} is an identity for the sum of the entire
physical fiber.

For endpoint sizes $m$ and $m+d$, define
\[
  R_{m,d}(h)
  :=
  \frac{\binom mh}{(d+1)(d+2)\cdots(d+h)}
  2^{-hm+h(h+1)/2},
  \qquad R_{m,d}(0):=1.
\]
The local partial-to-full ratio is exactly $R_{m,d}(h)$.  The only nonlocal
change is the ambient falling-factorial denominator:
\[
  \frac{(n)_{J+H}}{(n)_J}
  =(n-J)_H
  \le n^H.
\]
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-global-deficit-comparison}
  \frac{w(P,m-h)}{w_{\mathrm{full}}(P)}
  \le
  \prod_{e\in P}n^{h_e}R_{m_e,d_e}(h_e).
\end{equation}
The factor $n^H$ is paid once, after the physical fibers have been summed.

\subsection{Summing all deficits and regrouping by endpoint table}

The high-cell condition implies $2h<m$.  For such $h$,
\[
  h\left\lfloor\frac{3m-1}{4}\right\rfloor
  \le
  hm-\frac{h(h+1)}2,
\]
and therefore
\[
  n^hR_{m,d}(h)
  \le
  \left(
    \frac{nm}{2^{\lfloor(3m-1)/4\rfloor}}
  \right)^h.
\]
Let
\[
  \rho_e:=
  \frac{nm_e}{2^{\lfloor(3m_e-1)/4\rfloor}}.
\]
For all sufficiently large $n$, every $\rho_e$ is at most $1/2$, and hence
\[
  \sum_{h\ge 1}\rho_e^h
  \le 2\rho_e.
\]
The optional-deficit choices factor over the distinguishable cells of $P$:
\[
  \sum_{h:\,m-h\text{ high}}w(P,m-h)
  \le
  w_{\mathrm{full}}(P)
  \prod_{e\in P}(1+2\rho_e).
\]

The product on the right depends on $P$ only through its endpoint-type table
$L(P)$.  The exact weighted regrouping identity therefore gives
\[
  \sum_P w_{\mathrm{full}}(P)F(L(P))
  =
  \sum_{L\ \mathrm{realized}}W(L)F(L)
\]
for every nonnegative table function $F$.  Taking
\[
  F(L)=
  \prod_{i,j}(1+2\rho_{ij})^{L_{ij}}
\]
reduces the full high-skeleton sum to a finite endpoint-table sum.  The endpoint
transportation estimate and the partial-diagonal bound then imply
\[
  \operatorname{BareSkeletonSum}_n
  \le
  \exp\!\left\{
    o\!\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^4}\right)
  \right\}.
\]

\subsection{Residual attachment by injective restriction}

Fix the exposed high-support matching $M$.  Deletion of $M$ is injective on the
family of even residual edge sets.  Indeed, if two such edge sets have the same
restriction outside $M$, then their symmetric difference is an even subset of
a matching and must be empty.  Hence, for nonnegative activities $(q_e)$,
\[
  \sum_{F\ \mathrm{even}}
    \prod_{e\in F\setminus M}q_e
  \le
  \prod_{e\notin M}(1+q_e).
\]
The local increment activity is bounded by the same $q$ activity.  A direct
two-regime estimate therefore yields a deterministic sequence
$\eta_n\to0$ such that
\[
  \operatorname{AttachmentSum}_n
  \le
  \operatorname{BareSkeletonSum}_n
  \exp\!\left\{
    \eta_n\frac{n}{(\log n)^4}
  \right\}.
\]
Combining this estimate with the preceding bare-skeleton bound proves the
required normalized second-moment estimate.

\subsection{Amplification and final assembly}

Paley--Zygmund gives a positive-probability signed witness.  Section~10 applies
bounded differences to the largest induced subgraph that admits the prescribed
number of cocoloring classes.  A simultaneous leftover-coloring lemma then
shows that the vertices outside this induced subgraph require only
$o(n/(\log n)^3)$ additional classes.  This gives a high-probability upper
bound for $\zeta(G_n)$.  The ordinary first-moment calculation gives the
corresponding high-probability lower bound for $\chi(G_n)$.  A union bound
intersects the two events and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-v2}.

\section*{Guide to the proof}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Guide to the proof}
\label{sec:proof-architecture-v3}

The argument has three stages---location, existence, and amplification---but
the existence stage contains most of the new combinatorics. This guide records
both the mathematical mechanism and the deterministic error passed from one
stage to the next.

\subsection*{1. Locating two first-moment roots}

Let $r_+(n)$ be the continuous root of the unrestricted coloring profile, and
let $r_4^{\mathrm{co}}(n)$ be the root of the signed objective restricted to
the four deficits $2,3,4,5$. Sections~2 and~3 first isolate the phase and finite
dual errors
\[
  \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{ph}},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{dual}},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_{n,A}^{\mathrm{slope}},
\]
all tending to zero uniformly across the complete phase. At the unrestricted
root, Section~5 transports the finite target
$T_+(n)=\alpha-n/r_+(n)$ to the phase center $T_0$ through
\[
  \omega_n^{\mathrm{root}}
  =2\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{dual}}
   +2C_{\mathrm{Lip}}\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{target}}
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
No limiting functional is substituted before the exact finite support
comparison has been made.

If
\[
  A_4(\delta):=\log 2-D_4(\delta),
\]
where $D_4$ is the entropy loss caused by the four-point restriction, the root
comparison gives
\[
  r_+(n)-r_4^{\mathrm{co}}(n)
  =
  \left[
    \frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}A_4(\delta_n)
    +O(\omega_n^{\mathrm{root}})
  \right]
  \frac{n}{(\log n)^3}.
\]
The signed witness is placed at a tangent-rounded midpoint of these two roots.
The tangent correction preserves both conservation laws and does not alter the
fixed total number of classes. The retained separation is therefore one half
of the leading root gap, up to a deterministic error negligible on the target
scale. The final constant ledger proves the phase-uniform certificate
\[
  A_4(\delta)
  >\log\!\left(\frac{20000}{12777}\right)
  =\log\!\left(\frac{1000}{639}\right)
   +\log\!\left(\frac{12780}{12777}\right).
\]
The final summand is the fixed slack used to absorb the asymptotic error.

\subsection*{2. Extracting the exact overlap structure}

For two ordered signed witnesses, let $r=(r_{ab})$ be their overlap table. The
sum over compatible sign assignments is evaluated before any estimate is
made:
\[
  \operatorname{wt}_{\mathrm{sign}}(r)
  =
  \left(\prod_{a,b}g(r_{ab})\right)2^{\beta(H(r))}.
\]
The product is local; the factor $2^{\beta(H(r))}$ is the cardinality of a
binary cycle space. This identity is the point at which the signed problem
separates into cellwise rewards and a topological residual factor.

Section~7 handles all marked common subprofiles before high cells are
estimated. Its empty, central, and full ranges are disjoint and exhaustive.
Their deterministic errors are combined as
\[
  \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{pd}}
  :=e^{\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{empty}}}-1
    +\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{central}}
    +\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{full}}
  \longrightarrow0,
\]
so that the complete one-sided reference mass is at most
$1+\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{pd}}$.

In every remaining table, a cell above half the class-size cap is unique in its
row and column. Thus all high cells form a canonical block matching; there is
no auxiliary skeleton choice and no overlap is assigned to two skeletons.

\subsection*{3. Summing high cells before estimating them}

Fix a high-cell support $P$ and realized multiplicities
$j_e=m_e-h_e$. Because $P$ is a matching, the complete physical fiber has the
exact aggregate
\[
  w(P,j)
  =
  \frac{\prod_{e\in P}(s_e)_{j_e}(t_e)_{j_e}}
       {(n)_{\sum_ej_e}\prod_{e\in P}j_e!}
  \prod_{e\in P}g(j_e).
\]
Only after this fiber has been summed do we compare $j_e$ with the
full-containment multiplicity $m_e$. The local ratios factor, while the sole
nonlocal change is
\[
  \frac{(n)_{\sum_em_e}}{(n)_{\sum_ej_e}}
  =(n-\textstyle\sum_ej_e)_{\sum_eh_e}
  \le n^{\sum_eh_e}.
\]
Thus the finite-population loss is paid once rather than once per cell.

The positive deficits are summed by a finite optional-choice product. The
full-containment references are then partitioned exactly by endpoint table,
and the table sum is transported to the one-sided partial-diagonal weights.
With
\[
  \tau_n^{\mathrm{end}}
  =2^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{(n+1)(\alpha-2)}{2^{\alpha-2}}},
\]
the endpoint row sum is at most $1+3\tau_n^{\mathrm{end}}$. Section~9 therefore
defines the explicit bare-skeleton logarithmic cost
\[
\begin{split}
  \Gamma_n^{\mathrm{skel}}
  :={}&K\log\!\left(1+(\alpha+1)\rho_{16}\right)\\
      &+K\log\!\left(1+3\tau_n^{\mathrm{end}}\right)
       +\log\!\left(1+\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{pd}}\right).
\end{split}
\]
Its normalized form satisfies
\[
  \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{skel}}
  :=\frac{(\log n)^4}{n}\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{skel}}
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
The fiber identity, deficit product, and reference regrouping are valid for any
finite endpoint alphabet; only the phase estimate is specialized to four
consecutive sizes.

\subsection*{4. Restricting residual cycles and amplifying the seed}

After the high cells are exposed, Section~9 assigns one activity $q_{ab}$ to
each residual cell. Expanding the local rewards produces a weighted sum over
even residual edge sets. Deleting the exposed matching is injective on this
family: the symmetric difference of two sets with the same restriction would
be an even subset of a matching, and hence empty. The residual cycle-space sum
is therefore bounded by a full product.

The zero-residual case has attachment factor one. In the intrinsic regime the
attachment factor is at most $e^{C_{\mathrm{att}}U^2}$; in the complementary
regime it is at most
$\exp\{(\log 2)U2^{U/3}/2\}$. Accordingly, define
\[
  \Gamma_n^{\mathrm{att}}
  :=\max\!\left\{
      C_{\mathrm{att}}U^2,
      \frac{\log 2}{2}U2^{U/3}
    \right\},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{att}}
  :=\frac{(\log n)^4}{n}\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{att}}.
\]
Then $\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{att}}\to0$ uniformly in the exposed skeleton.
The exact conditional decomposition therefore gives
\[
  1
  \le
  \frac{\mathbb E Z^2}{(\mathbb E Z)^2}
  \le
  \exp\{\Lambda_n\},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_n
  :=\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{skel}}+\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{att}}
  =o\!\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^4}\right).
\]
The two summands have disjoint responsibilities: the first pays partial
diagonals, deficits, and endpoint transport; the second pays residual local
rewards and cycle space.

Paley--Zygmund supplies a witness with probability at least $e^{-\Lambda_n}$.
Section~10 then uses vertex-block bounded differences and a simultaneous
leftover-coloring estimate to turn this rare seed into a high-probability
cocoloring at a cost $o(n/(\log n)^3)$. Intersecting it with the ordinary
chromatic lower-tail event completes the gap estimate.

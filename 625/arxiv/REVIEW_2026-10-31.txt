The main theorem concerns G_n ~ G(n,1/2) through every integer n. It proves
a high-probability chromatic–cochromatic gap at least

  c n/(log n)^3,
  c = ((log 2)^2/4) log(200/153)
    = 0.03217587169793604... .

The stronger phase-resolved lower bound has leading coefficient
((log 2)^2/4) A_4(delta_n), with a deterministic error epsilon_n -> 0.
The phase constants are uniform, including sequences approaching integer
cutoffs. The new remark makes the rate explicit:

  epsilon_n = O(log log n / log n),

with a phase-independent implied constant. Equivalently, the remainder in
the unnormalized gap is O(n log log n/(log n)^4).

Why the refinement is justified
-------------------------------
Write L = log n and ell = log log n. The explicit overlap ledger in Section 9
already gives Lambda_n = O(n^(3/4) L). The amplification scales in (10.7)
therefore satisfy

  b_n = O(n^(7/8) L^(-1/2)),
  a_n = O(n^(15/16) L^(-7/4)) = o(n ell/L^4).

The root comparison (5.11) has error O(n ell/L^4), while the chromatic lower
location (4.1), rounded cocoloring location (5.13), and amplification cost
are smaller on this scale. Thus the normalized root error theta_n is
O(ell/L), and the normalized remaining cost eta_n is o(ell/L).
The already-defined epsilon_n = |theta_n| + eta_n has the asserted rate.

This result is stated and proved after the main theorem proof, with an
introductory pointer. It does not change the fixed uniform coefficient,
provide a numerical finite-n threshold, establish a matching upper bound,
or add a newly formalized Lean theorem. It exposes information already
contained in the written proof's explicit estimates.

A proper coloring partitions the vertices of a graph into independent sets; a cocoloring allows both independent sets and cliques. The minimum numbers of classes are the chromatic number $\chi(G)$ and cochromatic number $\zeta(G)$, respectively. Thus $\zeta(G)\le\chi(G)$. For $G_n\sim G(n,1/2)$, Erdos and Gimbel asked whether $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)$ tends to infinity with high probability. We prove $ \mathbb P( \chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\ge \frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}\log(\frac{200}{153}) \frac{n}{(\log n)^3} )\longrightarrow1. $ All logarithms are natural, and the limit holds as $n\to\infty$ through all integers. This lower bound has the conjectured scale $n/(\log n)^3$.

The natural threshold for class sizes need not be an integer. To cover every change caused by rounding, we use four consecutive sizes, with proportions chosen to separate the first-moment thresholds for colorings and cocolorings. We count partitions whose classes are marked as cliques or independent sets and estimate the contribution of their overlaps to the second moment. The resulting estimate gives a cocoloring with a possibly small probability. Amplification raises this to high probability, using only $o(n/(\log n)^3)$ extra classes. The fractional part of the class-size threshold is the rounding phase. Retaining this phase in the optimization gives a sharper explicit coefficient.

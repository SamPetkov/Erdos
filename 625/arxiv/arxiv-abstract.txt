A proper coloring partitions the vertices of a graph into independent sets. In a cocoloring, each class may be an independent set or a clique. The minimum numbers of classes are the chromatic number $\chi(G)$ and cochromatic number $\zeta(G)$, respectively. Thus $\zeta(G)\le\chi(G)$. For $G_n\sim G(n,1/2)$, Erdos and Gimbel asked whether $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)$ tends to infinity with high probability. We prove $ \mathbb P( \chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\ge \frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}\log(\frac{200}{153}) \frac{n}{(\log n)^3} )\longrightarrow1. $ All logarithms are natural, and the limit holds as $n\to\infty$ through all integers. This lower bound has the conjectured scale $n/(\log n)^3$.

We choose four consecutive class sizes below the real-valued first-moment center for independent sets. The fractional part of this center is the rounding phase. Optimizing the proportions at each phase gives a sharper explicit coefficient, with a deterministic error tending to zero uniformly over the phase. For the second moment, we count partitions with each class declared independent or complete, and bound the contribution from pairs of overlapping partitions. This gives a cocoloring with a possibly small probability. Concentration and a coloring of the leftover vertices then give high probability at a cost of $o(n/(\log n)^3)$ extra classes.

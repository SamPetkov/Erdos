The cochromatic number $\zeta(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the least number of parts in a partition of $V(G)$ into cliques and independent sets, so $\zeta(G)\le\chi(G)$. Erd\H{o}s and Gimbel asked whether $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\to\infty$ with high probability for $G_n\sim G(n,1/2)$. Heckel answered this affirmatively for about 95% of all integers $n$ and conjectured that the natural order of the difference along the full sequence is $n/(\log n)^3$. We prove that, as $n\to\infty$ through all integers, $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\ge c\,n/(\log n)^3$ with high probability, where $c=\frac14(\log 2)^2\log(8750/6681)\approx0.0324$ and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. The failure probabilities are summable, so under any coupling of the graphs $G_n$ the bound holds almost surely for all sufficiently large $n$. A phase-resolved refinement replaces $\log(8750/6681)$ by $A_5(\delta_n)-o(1)$, where $\delta_n$ is the fractional part of the first-moment approximation $\alpha_0(n)$ to the independence number and $A_5$ is an explicit, continuous, nonconstant function satisfying $A_5>\log(8750/6681)$ on $[0,1]$.

The proof is a second-moment argument for partitions with five consecutive class sizes, in which each class is declared independent or complete. We control all overlaps between two such partitions, including whole shared classes and large intersections of unequal classes, uniformly in the rounding phase. Vertex-exposure concentration, together with a coloring of the leftover vertices, then raises the resulting probability bound to high probability at a cost of $o(n/(\log n)^3)$ extra classes.

A proper coloring partitions the vertices of a graph into independent sets; a cocoloring allows both independent sets and cliques. The minimum numbers of classes are the chromatic number $\chi(G)$ and cochromatic number $\zeta(G)$, respectively. Thus $\zeta(G)\le\chi(G)$. For $G_n\sim G(n,1/2)$, Erdos and Gimbel asked whether $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)$ tends to infinity with high probability. We prove $ \mathbb P( \chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\ge \frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}\log(\frac{200}{153}) \frac{n}{(\log n)^3} )\longrightarrow1. $ All logarithms are natural, and the limit holds as $n\to\infty$ through all integers. This lower bound has the conjectured scale $n/(\log n)^3$.

We use four consecutive class sizes below the real first-moment center for independent-set sizes. This center need not be an integer; its fractional part is the rounding phase. Optimizing the proportions gives a sharper explicit phase-dependent coefficient, with one deterministic error tending to zero uniformly over the phase. We count partitions whose classes are marked as cliques or independent sets and estimate the contribution of their overlaps to the second moment. The estimate gives a cocoloring with a possibly small probability. Amplification raises this to high probability using $o(n/(\log n)^3)$ extra classes.
